## Research question
Can a bare binary admissibility relation, without additional relational information, uniquely determine the operational transition modes needed by the proposed loop—especially FOLD versus UNFOLD and SLOWDOWN versus CONTINUE?

## Hypothesis under test
If two transition systems expose exactly the same admissibility graph to the resolver, then that graph alone cannot select different modes based on representation ordering or dynamic proximity to a boundary, because those facts are not encoded in the graph.

## Method
Construct two pairs of indistinguishable inputs:

1. Same admissible edge, but opposite representation ordering. The desired interpretation differs between FOLD and UNFOLD.
2. Same admissible edge, but different dynamic/boundary margin. Under an explicitly illustrative policy, one case calls for SLOWDOWN and the other for CONTINUE.

The script checks that the bare relation signatures are identical while the additional relations differ.

## Results
- Case 1: PASS — admissibility alone cannot distinguish FOLD from UNFOLD without a representation-order relation.
- Case 2: PASS — admissibility alone cannot distinguish SLOWDOWN from CONTINUE without dynamic/boundary information.
- Overall: 2/2 constructed counterexample checks passed.

## Interpretation
This is a negative result for the sufficiency of a **bare** admissibility relation. It does not refute a richer unified transition law. It indicates that a candidate law must expose more than a yes/no admissibility predicate: at minimum, it needs access to representation relations and relevant dynamic/boundary information. These can be expressed relationally without committing to a fixed list of semantic coordinates, but that claim remains to be formalized.

## Limits
- Constructed counterexamples only; no empirical workload.
- No actual MCTS/UCT implementation.
- No proof that the added relations are minimal or universal.
- Illustrative margin values are not calibrated thresholds.
- Time/dynamics is represented only through an illustrative margin, not a validated temporal evolution model.

## Decision
**Do not mark the unified-law hypothesis VERIFIED or ADOPTED.** The next research step is to specify a structured transition contract that separates: (a) proposal preference, (b) admissibility, (c) representation ordering, and (d) dynamic/boundary evolution. Then test whether operational modes can be derived from that contract with fewer independent hand-coded rules and across multiple state representations.
