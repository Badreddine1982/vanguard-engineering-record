## Question
Can one domain-neutral transition kernel preserve separation between proposal preference, admissibility, activation, representation change, unresolved states, and dynamic horizon across two differently represented toy systems, without embedding domain coordinates or separate action-selection rules for each outcome?

## Design
The kernel consumes relation facts prepared by adapters: hard-constraint status (`TRUE`, `FALSE`, or `UNKNOWN`), capability availability, activation authorization, abstract representation comparison, proposed horizon, maximum admissible horizon, and revalidation outcome for the effective transition. It does not infer validity from preference. The outcome labels are observations emitted after resolving the relation set.

Two fixtures are used: (1) a ranked numeric representation and (2) a symbolic partial-order representation with a distinct structural admissibility rule. The same kernel runs both. A metamorphic relabeling test renames symbolic state/action identifiers without changing their relational facts. A tie test ensures equal-scored valid alternatives are not broken by lexical identifier order.

## Results
13/13 constructed checks passed:
- inadmissible high-preference proposal cannot override the constraint;
- valid alternatives remain available for branching;
- dynamic horizon reduction yields SLOWDOWN only when the effective transition passes revalidation;
- representation relation yields FOLD/UNFOLD observations;
- no admissible successor yields scoped TERMINATE;
- validity does not imply activation;
- same kernel handles the symbolic fixture;
- irrelevant relabeling preserves the canonical behavioral signature;
- failed revalidation blocks activation;
- UNKNOWN admissibility blocks activation and yields HOLD_UNRESOLVED rather than a false claim of impossibility;
- equal-score valid alternatives are retained without identifier-based tie-breaking; missing capability blocks activation.

## Interpretation
The run supports interface consistency on constructed cases. It is stronger than the six hard-coded mode examples of EXP-001 but still does not establish that the candidate contract is minimal, universal, or dimension-independent. The selector is deterministic and is not MCTS/UCT. The hard-admissibility, maximum-horizon, and effective-revalidation facts are supplied by the test adapter; their correctness is not proven by the kernel.

## Limits and next step
There is no empirical workload, learned state model, machine-checked formal proof, or external validation. A separate informal conditional proof-sketch file identifies the first kernel safety lemmas. Formalization must separately prove safety under explicit assumptions, specify adapter soundness obligations, establish meaningful conditions for dynamic step reduction and fold/unfold preservation, and distinguish safety from liveness/fair exploration. MCTS/UCT integration remains a later isolated experiment.
